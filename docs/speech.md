# Speech

Status: approved product change (C10, 2026-10-09), in progress: the generation pipeline (C10a), playback (C10b), and authoring (C10c) are implemented; the production release is pending. Phases are tracked in the [roadmap](roadmap.md#course-phases); [SPEECH_PLAN.md](../SPEECH_PLAN.md) holds the delivery checklist.

Speech lets a learner hear a lesson's new words, example sentences, and dialogues read aloud in the group's target language. Audio is synthesized by Azure AI Speech neural voices, generated once per distinct text in the background, stored in R2, and played from there. It builds on [lesson documents](courses.md#lesson-documents) and [new words](courses.md#new-words-and-recap).

## What is spoken

| Item | Text spoken | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Word term | the word's `term`, as [spoken text](#spoken-text), or its [IPA](#pronunciation-override) when set | narrator |
| Word example | the word's `example` | narrator |
| Example block | the sentence's plain text (inline styles and links dropped); not the translation or note | narrator |
| Dialogue turn | the turn's `text`; never the speaker label | the speaker's [cast](#dialogue-cast) voice |

Nothing else in a lesson is spoken: not forms, meanings, notes, callouts, paragraphs, practice prompts, posts, or comments. Outside lessons, the [AI tutor](ai-tutor.md) has [on-demand audio](#on-demand-audio) (C14).

## Voices

Voices are a fixed list per target language in the shared contracts, with one narrator voice per language:

| Language | Narrator | Other voices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dutch (`nl`) | `nl-NL-FennaNeural` | `nl-NL-ColetteNeural`, `nl-NL-MaartenNeural` |
| German (`de`) | `de-DE-KatjaNeural` | `de-DE-AmalaNeural`, `de-DE-ConradNeural`, `de-DE-KillianNeural` |

Only Netherlands and Germany voices are listed; Belgian, Austrian, and Swiss voices are left out for accent consistency. Changing the list is a product change.

## Dialogue cast

Characters recur across a course's lessons, so a course has a cast: a map from speaker label to voice, edited by the course owner with the course details. Speaker labels match trimmed and case-insensitively, so a cast may not hold two labels that differ only in case or surrounding space; labels are stored trimmed. A speaker without a cast entry takes the voice at their position among the dialogue's speakers in order of first appearance (cast speakers included), counted from the voice after the narrator and wrapping round the whole list, narrator included: in Dutch the first speaker is Colette, the second Maarten, the third Fenna. A cast voice of another language is ignored. Changing the cast regenerates the affected turns in the background; the old clips stay valid for any other course that uses them.

The owner edits the cast under Dialogue voices in the course details form (C10c):

- It lists the course's speakers: every dialogue speaker of every lesson's published and draft documents, trimmed, once per cast key, in order of first appearance, followed by cast entries no dialogue uses any more, so the owner can clear them. Each speaker has a voice select with Automatic (no cast entry) and the group language's voices, the narrator labelled as such.
- Every voice of the group language has a sample button that plays a fixed sentence (*Hallo! Zo klinkt mijn stem.* in Dutch, *Hallo! So klingt meine Stimme.* in German) once its clip is ready. Samples are ordinary content-addressed clips, generated by the worker, never on request.
- The form sends the cast only once the editor has loaded, so a failed load leaves the cast unchanged and says so. A course without dialogue speakers says so instead of listing selects.

## Spoken text

A term is written for reading, not for speaking. A shared contracts helper turns it into the text that is spoken, and every caller uses that helper:

- Text in parentheses is dropped: `iets (inf.)` → `iets`.
- `/` and `·` between alternatives become a pause: `jij / je` → `jij, je`.
- `…` is dropped and whitespace collapses.
- Articles stay: `de keuken` is spoken with its article.
- Example sentences and dialogue turns are spoken as written.

Speech is generated without context: the same text and voice always produce the same clip, whichever lesson or group it comes from. Sentences carry their own intonation. A bare word is read in isolation, which is right except for words whose stress depends on meaning; those take a pronunciation override.

## Pronunciation override

A vocabulary word has an optional `ipa` field: an IPA transcription of the term, for words the voice would stress or pronounce wrongly, such as *voorkomen* ("to occur", `ˈvoːrkoːmə`) against *voorkomen* ("to prevent", `voːrˈkoːmə`). Wiktionary gives a transcription for most words.

- At most 100 characters, limited by the contracts to IPA letters and diacritics, stress and length marks, `.`, and spaces. Anything else is refused, so the field can never inject markup into the synthesis request.
- It applies to the word's term clip only. Example sentences and dialogue turns are never overridden.
- Authors edit it in the word's form, collapsed under "Pronunciation" like forms, example, and note. Learners never see it; they only hear its effect.
- An empty field means none.

## Generation

Lesson generation is strictly background work. Opening or playing a lesson never calls Azure. Synthesis on request is limited to the tutor's [on-demand audio](#on-demand-audio) and the approved [lesson activity role-play](#lesson-activity-role-play) surface.

- **Clip identity.** A clip is identified by the SHA-256 hash of its schema version, voice, and the exact synthesis markup (spoken text or IPA). Its R2 key is `speech/{hash}.mp3`. Identical text with the same voice shares one clip across lessons, courses, and groups, so Azure is called once per distinct clip.
- **Jobs.** Each lesson has at most one pending speech job. Publishing a lesson makes its job due at once; saving a draft makes it due 2 minutes after the last save, so text typed in between is never synthesized; changing a course's cast makes the job of every lesson in the course due at once.
- **Worker.** A Cron Trigger runs every minute. It takes due jobs, collects the lesson's spoken items from its published document and its draft, and claims every clip that is not ready by inserting its row; a row that already exists is never synthesized again. It then synthesizes each claimed clip, writes the MP3 to R2, and marks the row ready. Each run synthesizes a bounded number of clips and leaves the rest of the job due for the next run. Publishing also starts one run in the background after its response. Unless Azure stopped the run, the worker then makes sure every voice sample of both languages is ready; once they are, this is a single lookup.
- **Failures.** A failed clip is retried after 1, 2, 4, and 8 minutes and marked failed after 5 attempts. An Azure `429` response stops the whole run and pushes the job back by its `Retry-After`, or a minute. A claim that never finishes expires after 10 minutes and can be claimed again.
- **Retention.** Clips are content-addressed and shared, so editing, unpublishing, or deleting a lesson never deletes them. A deleted lesson's job is deleted with it.
- Leading and trailing silence is removed in the synthesis request (`mstts:silence` `Leading-exact` and `Tailing-exact`), so a word clip is about as long as the word. Azure still pads some short clips to about 1.9 seconds with trailing silence; playback is unaffected, since nothing follows a clip automatically except within a playing dialogue.
- **Limits.** A run takes at most 10 due jobs and synthesizes at most 40 clips. A job waiting only on a retry or on another run's claim is pushed to that time instead of staying due. An Azure `401` or `403` (a refused key) stops the run like a `429`, without counting an attempt. Without `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` and `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION`, the worker does nothing and jobs stay due.

## Playback

- A speaker button follows each spoken item whose clip is ready; while a clip is pending or failed, its item shows no button. Read responses carry each ready item's clip URL, so the web never probes R2.
- **Where:** word terms in every New words list (the player's panel, the words step, the reader's word list, and the lesson page's New words panel) and on both sides of [word cards](words.md#word-cards) (lesson recap and course recap), on the Words tab's [word rows](words.md#words-tab), and on [drill cards](words.md#a-card) (the term on an Understand front and on every revealed back); word examples on the card's back and in an opened New words row; example blocks in the reader and player; and each dialogue turn.
- A dialogue with ready turns starts with a transport row that plays its ready turns in order and marks the turn being spoken:
  - **Play dialogue** starts at the first ready turn. While it plays the button reads Pause dialogue and pauses the clip where it is; the paused turn stays marked, and Resume dialogue carries on from that point through the rest of the dialogue.
  - **Previous line** and **Next line** start the previous or next ready turn and carry on from there, also from a paused dialogue. Previous on the first turn starts it again; Next is disabled on the last turn, and both are disabled before the dialogue starts.
  - **Dialogue progress** is a bar with one equal segment per ready turn: finished turns are filled, the current turn fills as its clip plays, and later turns are empty. It is a slider: pressing a segment, or Home, End, and the arrow keys, starts that turn and carries on from there, also before the dialogue has started. A mono count (*2 / 5*) follows it.
  - **Stop dialogue** ends the dialogue at once, playing or paused, and clears the marked turn and the bar; the next Play dialogue starts at the first turn. It is disabled before the dialogue starts.
  - The dialogue finishes after its last ready turn and returns to Play dialogue. Any other speaker button, including a turn's own, stops it.
  - In the player, where lines arrive one step at a time, it plays only the lines shown so far, so it never reads ahead.
- One clip plays at a time across the page; starting another stops the current one. A speaker button is pressed while its clip loads or plays, and pressing it again stops it. Leaving the surface that started a clip (closing a word's details, moving to another player step, closing the player) stops it. Playback never autoplays, with one exception: revealing a [word card](words.md#word-cards) or a [drill card](words.md#a-card) plays its term, as part of the learner's own Show meaning or Show answer press, while the reveal sound is on.
- A clip the browser cannot play shows "The audio could not be played. Try again." beside its button for five seconds.
- The web keeps one `Audio` element for the page (`apps/web/src/molecules/Speech.tsx`); surfaces provide a speech scope that resolves item keys to URLs, from the lesson's `speech` (or a draft preview's ready `draftSpeech` items), while indexed words (the course recap and the Words tab) carry their own `speech`.
- Learners hear published content. The owner and active contributors also hear their draft and preview, once its job has run.
- The vocabulary form shows each word's audio status from `draftSpeech`: Audio ready with a speaker button, Audio pending, or failed. A word whose spoken term or IPA differs from its saved draft, or whose item has no status yet, is pending; after a save, items whose voice, text, or IPA changed become pending at once and the rest keep their status. While any draft item is pending, the editor reads the lesson again every 15 seconds; the editor content never follows these reads.
- The vocabulary form's optional Pronunciation (IPA) field is validated by the contracts as the author types. A value they refuse stays in the field with a message and is not saved until it is valid; clearing the field removes the override.
- Every label is an i18next key, such as "Play pronunciation of {{term}}" or "Line {{number}} of {{total}}, {{speaker}}".

## On-demand audio

C14 (approved product change, 2026-10-15). The [AI tutor](ai-tutor.md) speaks target-language text that is not in any lesson, so its clips are synthesized when a learner asks for them.

- **What can be spoken.** Only text the server has stored: a target-language span the model marked in one of the learner's own assistant messages, the sentence of a tutor Say it or Dictation component, and the sentence of an [AI practice](courses.md#ai-practices) dictation. The request names the message and span index (`{ spanIndex }`, counted in order across the message's text parts), or the message and the component's tool call (`{ toolCallId }`), or the practice (`POST /api/groups/:groupId/courses/:courseId/lessons/:lessonId/ai-practices/:practiceId/speech`, no body, dictation practices of a published lesson the account can read; C14h); the API reads the text itself and never accepts text from the client, so nothing a learner types is ever spoken. A span is at most 300 characters; longer spans show no speaker button and are refused with `400 SPAN_TOO_LONG`. A span index past the message's spans, or a tool call that is not a Say it or Dictation component, is `404 SPAN_NOT_FOUND`; a user message is `404 MESSAGE_NOT_FOUND`.
- **Voice.** The library language's narrator voice, with the same [spoken text](#spoken-text) rules and no pronunciation override.
- **Caching.** A clip has the same [identity](#generation) as a lesson clip (schema version, voice, and markup), so a sentence that a lesson already has is served from R2 at once without calling Azure, and a clip made on demand is reused by every later request and by lessons. The API claims the clip row as the worker does, synthesizes it synchronously within the request (15-second timeout), and returns `{ url }`; a clip that another request (or the worker) holds, or one waiting for a retry, answers `202 { status: "pending" }` and the web retries after a second, at most five times. A synthesis that fails, or a clip that failed for good, answers `503 SPEECH_FAILED`.
- **Metering.** Every served request writes a `speech_usage` row (account, surface `ai_message` for the tutor, `ai_practice` for an AI practice dictation, or `lesson_activity` for role-play, clip hash, characters of the spoken text, and whether it was cached); a `202` or a failure writes none. A cache hit costs nothing at Azure but still counts toward the account's daily **character cap**, set in the [AI settings](settings-and-administration.md#ai-settings) (default 5,000 characters per UTC day). The cap is checked before the clip is looked up: a request that would take the account past it is refused with `429 SPEECH_DAILY_LIMIT` and `resetsAt` (the next UTC midnight) and the speaker button says the daily audio limit is reached.
- **Playback.** Clips play through the shared `Speech` element under the [playback](#playback) rules: one at a time, never automatically. The speaker button shows a spinner while the clip is generated.
- Without the Azure key the routes answer `503 SPEECH_UNAVAILABLE` and the web hides the speaker buttons for the rest of the visit.

## Lesson activity role-play

v2 deployed to production 2026-10-21 ([ACTIVITIES_V2_PLAN.html](../ACTIVITIES_V2_PLAN.html)). [Lesson activities](lesson-activities.md#original-dialogue-role-play) adds original-dialogue playback in course cast voices and hold-to-talk Sofia turns. Activity speech resolves stored original turn/chunk IDs and uses the existing clip cache and on-demand allowance; arbitrary text/voices are not accepted. This is an additional on-demand surface alongside Tutor audio, whose narrator rule stays unchanged. Recording is active only while held; explicit Send may submit a private transcript under the [activity privacy exception](lesson-activities.md#speech-and-privacy-exception). In v2, activity speech also covers `read_aloud` and `speak_from_english` lines, resolved from stored line IDs as role-play turns are; see [lesson activities](lesson-activities.md).

- **Route.** `POST /api/courses/:courseId/lessons/:lessonId/activities/:activityId/speech` takes `{ runId, turnId, chunkIndex? }`. The turn must be one of the activity's stored turns (a partner line or Sofia's reference line), and it is spoken from its stored reference text. The voice is the course cast voice for its speaker, resolved as the lesson's own dialogue resolves it. The clip is the lesson's clip for that text and voice, with the same hash and R2 key, so a line the lesson's dialogue already has is served without calling Azure.
- **Parts.** A line longer than `ACTIVITY_SPEECH_CHUNK_MAX` (300 characters, the on-demand clip limit) is split by `speechChunks` (`apps/api/src/lessonActivities/speech.ts`) at sentence ends, then at spaces, and is never truncated. A multi-part line needs `chunkIndex`; a missing index on it, or an index past its last part, is `400 ACTIVITY_CHUNK_INVALID`.
- **Metering.** The daily character cap is checked before the clip is looked up. Each served part writes a `speech_usage` row with surface `lesson_activity`, cache hits included. A part that another request holds answers `202 { status: "pending", retryAfterMs }`; a failed synthesis answers `503 SPEECH_FAILED`; without the Azure key the route answers `503 SPEECH_UNAVAILABLE`.
- **Archived courses.** Only clips that are already ready are served. An archived course never synthesizes new audio, so an uncached part answers `409 ACTIVITY_READ_ONLY`.

## Privacy

Spoken lesson text and the tutor's stored target-language text are sent to Microsoft Azure AI Speech, and clips are public-by-URL like lesson images; see [security and privacy](security-and-privacy.md#lesson-speech). Post, comment, practice answer, and profile text, and anything a learner types, is never sent for synthesis.

## API and data

- Lesson reads (`GET .../lessons/:lessonId`, and the lessons a course read includes) add `speech`, a map from item key to clip URL for ready clips only. Item keys are `word:{wordId}`, `wordExample:{wordId}`, `example:{blockId}`, and `turn:{blockId}:{index}`. Editors also receive `draftSpeech` for the draft, with each item's status (`pending`, `ready`, or `failed`) and its URL once ready; an item whose clip has never been claimed is pending. Learners receive `draftSpeech: null`. The course read returns `speechCast` with the course.
- Course words and word bookmark responses add each word's `speech: { term, example }` URLs, `null` while not ready.
- The course details update (`PATCH .../courses/:courseId`) accepts an optional `speechCast`; leaving it out keeps the cast, and an empty map clears it. It is owner only like the rest of the details, and a voice outside the group language's list is refused with `400 SPEECH_VOICE_INVALID`.
- `GET .../courses/:courseId/speech-cast` returns the cast editor's data: `speakers` (as listed under [dialogue cast](#dialogue-cast)) and `voices`, the group language's voices narrator first, each with its `sample` URL or `null` while not ready. It is owner only (`403 COURSE_EDIT_FORBIDDEN`), and a course outside the group is `404`.
- No route accepts text to synthesize. Alongside the [lesson activity](#lesson-activity-role-play) route, direct synthesis uses the tutor's `POST /api/ai/conversations/:id/messages/:messageId/speech` with `{ spanIndex }` or `{ toolCallId }` (C14g), and the AI practice dictation route `POST .../lessons/:lessonId/ai-practices/:practiceId/speech` (C14h); both read stored text; see [on-demand audio](#on-demand-audio).
- Data lives in [`speech_clips`](data-model.md#speech_clips), [`speech_jobs`](data-model.md#speech_jobs), and, for on-demand audio, [`speech_usage`](data-model.md#speech_usage); the cast is a column of [`courses`](data-model.md#courses), and the IPA is part of the word payload and of [`course_lesson_words`](data-model.md#course_lesson_words).
- Speech recognition of spoken answers is not synthesis and is owned by [words](words.md#recognizers); its Microsoft option uses the same Azure resource.
- Azure is called through its REST endpoint from the Worker, with the key as the `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` secret and the region as the `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION` variable; see [operations](operations.md).
