# Dutch Foundations — Part V briefs: Talking about the past

Owner: content coordinator. Part of the [content docs](index.md). All five lessons are accepted; the lesson files and [ledger-part-v.md](ledger-part-v.md) record what was actually taught.

## Part V overview (rows 25–29)

Part V introduces talking about the past in the storyline after Sofia returns from Valencia to Utrecht on Sunday 3 January 2027. It may look back over her arrival, daily life, home, work, friends, Christmas and New Year. Keep the first steps concrete: familiar people and familiar activities make the new verb forms easier to notice.

### Known knowledge

Learners know the present tense, separable verbs, modal + infinitive, `aan het + infinitive`, `vinden dat`, and subordinate-clause word order with `dat` from Parts I–IV. They have seen past forms in vocabulary-card `forms` as reference data, but Parts II–IV have not taught productive past-tense grammar. The participle system is new.

### Part-wide scope and guardrails

- Introduce past meaning in row 25 with the perfect using **hebben + voltooid deelwoord**. Row 26 adds the perfect with **zijn**; row 27 teaches irregular participles and the `ge-` patterns; row 28 teaches the simple past; row 29 compares the perfect and simple past and develops sequencing.
- Row 25 uses only regular, separable or inseparable participles specifically listed in its brief. Do not use a `zijn` perfect, irregular participle, simple past, or explain general `ge-` spelling rules early.
- Row 26 introduces `zijn + voltooid deelwoord` for movement and change of state. A few common irregular participles are unavoidable; teach them as fixed forms only. The general irregular-participle patterns and wider `ge-` rules remain for row 27.
- Recycle known words where possible. New words still need cards under the style guide; do not repeat terms already listed in Parts I–IV unless a genuinely new meaning is taught and noted.
- Dutch should stay short, natural Netherlands Dutch at A1–early A2. English explanations may name the perfect and past participle, but define them with examples.
- Preserve the story timeline. The course opens after Sofia's return on Sunday 3 January 2027; details established in earlier lessons can be retold in the past. Avoid assigning an unestablished activity to yesterday.

## Row 25 — What did you do yesterday?

**Target:** Perfect tense with `hebben + voltooid deelwoord`.

**Story and continuity:** Monday 4 January 2027, the day after Sofia returns from Valencia to Utrecht on Sunday 3 January. She sees Noor and catches up about the holidays. Keep the present-day frame in the present, but let the characters retell familiar events from Part II–IV, especially Christmas, the housewarming, work and Dutch class. “Yesterday” is Sunday 3 January: Sofia has unpacked, made tea and tidied her room after arriving. Use those simple, plausible actions for the target practice, alongside `met kerst` recollections. Do not say how she travelled back; that perfect with `zijn` is row 26.

**Grammar facts to teach:**

- Form: finite present-tense `hebben` + past participle at the end: `Ik heb gewerkt.` / `Heb je gekookt?` / `Noor heeft gebeld.`
- Affirmative main clause: subject + conjugated `hebben` + other material + participle. In yes/no questions, `hebben` comes first.
- Regular weak participles: `ge-` + stem + `-t` or `-d`, chosen by the final consonant of the infinitive stem (`'t kofschip`/`'t fokschaap` may be referenced only if explained simply). Use transparent examples with known infinitives, such as `werken → gewerkt`, `maken → gemaakt`, `koken → gekookt`, `wonen → gewoond`, `leren → geleerd`, `bellen → gebeld`.
- Keep the vowel spelling in the stem where needed (`maken → gemaakt`, `wonen → gewoond`). A brief pattern is enough; do not turn this into an extensive spelling lesson.
- Separable verbs form the participle with the prefix around `ge`: `opbellen → opgebeld`, `opruimen → opgeruimd`, only if used and useful. Inseparable prefixes and their no-`ge` pattern are deferred to row 27.
- `hebben` is the helper verb for this lesson's selected verbs. Do not present the full auxiliary-choice system; row 26 teaches `zijn`.

**Required vocabulary and chunks:** Use `gisteren` and a natural Christmas time phrase; list them as new words only if not already known. Introduce a focused set of useful verbs in the infinitive with participle patterns in `forms`. Avoid `Wat heb je gisteren gedaan?` because `doen → gedaan` is irregular; ask `Wat heb je gisteren gemaakt/gekookt/geleerd?` instead. Reach the style guide's 15–35 new-word range with genuinely useful terms and chunks, while ensuring none repeats the earlier ledgers.

**Practice plan:** At least 7 blocks / 45 items: present `hebben` + regular participle fill-ins; participle spelling choice (`-t`/`-d`); complete a short dialogue; build word order with the participle last; present-to-perfect transformations; EN→NL and NL→EN translations; a reading passage about Sofia and Noor's catch-up with at least six comprehension questions; one short free-production prompt about yesterday or a recent day. Every item has an answer and a note wherever a rule, alternative or likely mistake applies.

**Required lesson features:** Follow the style guide's full skeleton, volume, dialogue, examples, callout and feature checklist. Include a grammar callout for `hebben + participle`; an important/hint callout for word order; a warning callout for putting the participle in the wrong position; and a pronunciation/spelling callout for `-t/-d`. Use the lesson colour convention consistently. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, at least ten translated examples, one nested list, one numbered list, a column list that compares infinitive and participle, 1–3 image ideas, and no more than one link.

**Off-limits:** Perfect with `zijn`; irregular participles (`gedaan`, `gezien`, `geweest`, `gehad`, etc.); the simple past; systematic inseparable-prefix rules; `ge-` on verbs ending in `-eren` or compounds; present perfect auxiliary selection; `toen` as a conjunction; any grammar from rows 26–29 not listed above. Avoid errors like `*Ik heb gewerkt gisteren` as learner production; it may appear only as a clearly marked wrong form followed immediately by its correction.

**Acceptance checks:** Story facts agree with [storyline.md](storyline.md); no row-26–29 grammar leaks in the story, reading, vocabulary forms or answers; vocabulary terms do not repeat earlier lesson terms; validator prints OK with at most 125 player steps; reviewer resolves every Dutch and exercise issue before acceptance.

## Row 26 — Where did you go?

**Target:** Perfect with `zijn + voltooid deelwoord` for movement and change of location/state.

**Story and continuity:** Tuesday 5 January 2027, Sofia and Noor continue their catch-up. Sofia tells Noor about her return to Utrecht on Sunday 3 January and about a short walk in Utrecht on Monday 4 January. The return mode is not established: do not invent a flight, train, bus, or other transport for the return. You may say `Sofia is teruggekomen in Utrecht` as a fixed useful form. Keep the current conversation in the present and retell only these established events. Noor may recount a familiar trip from earlier parts, but do not introduce a new dated story fact.

**Grammar facts to teach:**

- Form: present-tense `zijn` + the participle at the end: `Ik ben naar huis gegaan.` / `Noor is thuisgekomen.`
- Use a carefully bounded set of common movement/change verbs: `gaan → gegaan`, `komen → gekomen`, `aankomen → aangekomen`, `terugkomen → teruggekomen`, `vertrekken → vertrokken`, `blijven → gebleven`. Treat participles as whole forms to learn; do not explain irregular formation or general `ge-` patterns (row 27).
- Affirmative word order and yes/no question order recycle row 25: subject + finite `zijn` + rest + participle; in a yes/no question, `zijn` comes first. The participle goes at the end.
- State that this lesson's selected movement/change verbs use `zijn`; do not present a complete auxiliary-choice system or compare all verbs with `hebben`.

**Required vocabulary and chunks:** Recycle `gisteren` and known places. Introduce useful movement words/chunks such as `terugkomen`, `aankomen`, `vertrekken`, `weggaan`, `naar huis`, and `onderweg`, with correct `hij · past · participle` forms on verbs. Aim for 15–35 genuinely new terms, checking all prior part ledgers and lesson 25; do not repeat a term unless a genuinely new meaning is explained.

**Practice plan:** At least 7 blocks / 45 items: select `zijn` vs participle completion (with no general auxiliary-choice rule), participle recognition/recall for the listed verbs, short dialogue completion, word order with the participle last, present-to-perfect transformations, EN→NL and NL→EN translations, a reading passage about Sofia's Sunday return and Monday walk with at least six comprehension questions, and one free-production prompt about a journey or movement. Every item has an answer and a note wherever a rule, alternative or likely mistake applies.

**Required lesson features:** Follow the style guide's full skeleton, volume, dialogue, examples, callout and feature checklist. Include a grammar callout for `zijn + participle`; an important/hint callout for word order; a warning callout for putting the participle in the wrong position; and a hint/culture callout distinguishing destination `naar` from being at home only if useful. Use the lesson colour convention consistently. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, at least ten translated examples, one nested list, one numbered list, a column list comparing infinitive and participle, 1–3 image ideas, and no more than one link.

**Off-limits:** Simple past; broad irregular-participle or `ge-` rules (row 27); aspect/sequence comparison (row 29); `toen` as a conjunction; unestablished return transport; claims that all motion verbs necessarily use `zijn`; any grammar from later rows not listed above.

**Acceptance checks:** Story facts agree with [storyline.md](storyline.md); participles and `zijn` forms are natural and correct; no general auxiliary-choice rule or later grammar leaks in; vocabulary terms do not repeat earlier lessons; validator prints OK with at most 125 player steps; reviewer resolves every Dutch and exercise issue before acceptance.

## Row 27 — What have you seen and done?

**Target:** Common irregular past participles and the main `ge-` patterns, used in the perfect with the already-taught auxiliary forms.

**Story and continuity:** Wednesday 6 January 2027, Sofia and Noor continue their holiday catch-up at home. They can talk about familiar events from Christmas and earlier in Utrecht. Do not add a new dated trip or change established facts. Keep dialogue about lived experience in the perfect; do not teach simple-past forms or compare tenses (row 28/29).

**Grammar facts to teach:**

- Teach a small useful set of irregular participles as forms to learn, e.g. `doen → gedaan`, `zien → gezien`, `eten → gegeten`, `drinken → gedronken`, `lezen → gelezen`, `schrijven → geschreven`, `hebben → gehad`, `zijn → geweest`. Use correct auxiliary choice only for forms already established in rows 25–26; do not explain a full selection system.
- Explain regular `ge- + stem + -t/-d` as a review, then introduce three productive spelling patterns: no `ge-` with inseparable prefixes (`be-`, `ver-`, `ont-`); no extra `ge-` with verbs ending in `-eren`; separable verbs put `ge` between prefix and verb. Use a few clear, common examples; avoid a long exception list.
- Keep participles at the clause end and recycle question inversion. Do not teach simple past forms, `toen` sequencing, or a perfect-versus-simple-past rule.

**Required vocabulary and chunks:** Choose 15–35 genuinely useful new terms/chunks, with participles listed on verb cards. Check earlier ledgers and rows 25–26. Recycle known words and names rather than inventing a new story event.

**Practice and features:** At least 7 blocks / 45 items, including irregular participle recall, `ge-` pattern recognition, dialogue completion, word order, translations in both directions, and a reading with at least six questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, grammar/important/warning callouts as appropriate, a nested list, numbered list, column list, 1–3 image ideas, and no more than one link. Follow the style guide and validator cap of 125 steps.

**Off-limits:** Simple past and tense comparison; general auxiliary-choice explanations; obscure participle exceptions; new dated story facts; `toen` as a conjunction.

**Acceptance checks:** Forms and spelling patterns are correct and bounded; no row 28–29 grammar leaks in; no duplicate vocabulary; story continuity is exact; reviewer resolves Dutch and exercise issues.

## Row 28 — When you were younger

**Target:** Simple past (onvoltooid verleden tijd) of `zijn`, `hebben`, and a small set of common weak and strong verbs for past states and situations.

**Story and continuity:** Thursday 7 January 2027. In a relaxed conversation, Sofia and Noor describe familiar childhood routines and places: Sofia grew up in Valencia; Noor grew up in Groningen. Keep biographical details consistent with the cast and do not invent family facts. The lesson may use past-tense anecdotes but must not compare simple past with the perfect (row 29).

**Grammar facts to teach:**

- `zijn`: `was` / `waren`; `hebben`: `had` / `hadden`.
- Regular weak past: stem + `-te/-ten` or `-de/-den`, selected by the final stem sound (`'t kofschip`); distinguish singular and plural endings with clear examples.
- Add only a few high-frequency strong forms needed by the story (e.g. `gaan → ging/gingen`, `komen → kwam/kwamen`, `doen → deed/deden`). Present them as individual forms, not a full strong-verb taxonomy.
- Recycle present-time word order and familiar time expressions. Do not teach subordinate `toen` clauses or sequencing rules; that belongs to row 29.

**Required vocabulary and chunks:** 15–35 new terms/chunks, checked against all earlier ledgers. Childhood-related words must be useful and glossed; avoid unsupported claims about Sofia's or Noor's families.

**Practice and features:** At least 7 blocks / 45 items, including choose/build `was/waren` and `had/hadden`, weak past endings, a short dialogue, transformations from known present sentences into simple past, both translation directions, and a reading with six comprehension questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, a nested list, numbered list, column list, 1–3 image ideas, and no more than one link. Use grammar/important/warning callouts and follow all style-guide requirements; maximum 125 steps.

**Off-limits:** Perfect-tense comparison, rules for irregular participles (row 27), narrative sequencing or `toen` clauses (row 29), and invented character biography.

**Acceptance checks:** Conjugations, spelling and agreement are correct; examples stay within the selected verb set; there is no row 29 comparison; story and vocabulary are consistent; reviewer resolves all Dutch and exercise issues.

## Row 29 — First, then and after that

**Target:** Compare the perfect and simple past in everyday contexts and sequence a short account with `eerst`, `toen`, `daarna`, and `uiteindelijk`.

**Story and continuity:** Friday 8 January 2027. Sofia tells Noor about a real, already-established day in Utrecht—her return on 3 January, her walk on 4 January, or another event established in lessons 25–28. Use exact facts and no new timeline contradictions. This is the first lesson that explicitly compares the two past tenses.

**Grammar facts to teach:**

- Recycle the perfect forms from rows 25–27 and simple past from row 28. Explain a practical A1–A2 distinction with natural conversational examples: the perfect is common for completed events in conversation; the simple past is common for past states/background and in connected narration, with frequent forms such as `was`, `had`, and `ging`. Avoid presenting this as an absolute rule; both forms can occur depending on context.
- Sequence completed events with `eerst`, `toen`, `daarna`, and `uiteindelijk`. Teach `toen` as a time word in short, clear clauses; include subordinate word order only if expressly needed and explain it fully. Prefer main clauses to keep the focus on tense choice.
- Recycle V2 with a fronted sequencing word and use punctuation consistently.

**Required vocabulary and chunks:** Add 15–35 genuinely useful new terms/chunks, checking previous lessons. Sequence words count as new only if not already introduced; otherwise card new narrative vocabulary.

**Practice and features:** At least 7 blocks / 45 items, including choose-the-natural-tense in context with a note that explains alternatives, order events, complete a dialogue, rewrite a short sequence, both translation directions, and a reading with at least six comprehension questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, a nested list, numbered list, column list, 1–3 image ideas, and no more than one link. Follow style guide and maximum 125 steps.

**Off-limits:** Absolute rules that force one tense in every context; advanced literary narration; new story facts; complex subordinate clauses unless needed for a clearly taught `toen` pattern.

**Acceptance checks:** Tense choices are idiomatic and explanations allow valid alternatives; sequencing and V2 are correct; no vocabulary repeats; timeline is consistent; independent review resolves all Dutch and exercise issues.
