# Dutch Foundations — Part VI briefs: More complex Dutch

Owner: content coordinator. Part of the [content docs](index.md). These briefs plan lessons; accepted lesson files and [ledger-part-vi.md](ledger-part-vi.md) record what was taught.

## Part VI overview (rows 30–36)

Part VI expands learners' ability to explain, compare, connect and plan. Build on the present and past tenses, word order, modals, and the basic *dat*-clauses in Parts I–V. Keep each lesson focused; later rows own relative clauses (33), purpose infinitives (34), conditions and *zou/zouden* (35), and future choices (36).

### Known knowledge

Learners can form main-clause V2 and yes/no inversion; use *dat* with *vinden, denken, hopen,* and *zeggen* with the verb at the end; use past perfect and selected simple-past forms; form comparatives and use *dan*, *even/net zo/niet zo … als* from 18-b; use *te* in *hoeven … te* and positional verb + *te* constructions from Part IV.

### Part-wide guardrails

- Recycle previously taught structures and words. Every new vocabulary item follows the style guide and is checked against prior ledgers, especially Part V.
- Keep explanations in accessible English and model natural Netherlands Dutch. Each lesson must meet the style guide's lesson skeleton, feature and volume criteria, with no more than 125 player steps.
- Row 30 adds *omdat* and temporal *als* to the known *dat*-clauses. Row 31 contrasts *want* and *omdat*. Row 35 owns conditional *als … dan* and *zou/zouden*; don't introduce those in row 30.
- Row 32 extends the comparative from 18-b with superlatives. Don't teach relative clauses, purpose *om … te*, or future *zullen* early.
- Avoid inventing details about characters' work, studies, families, or schedules. Add fixed story facts to [storyline.md](storyline.md) only after acceptance.

## Row 30 — Work and study

**Target:** subordinate clauses with *omdat, dat,* and temporal *als*; verb-final order in the subordinate clause and main-clause order when the subordinate clause comes first.

**Story and continuity:** Monday 11 January 2027. Set a simple scene at Sofia's IT office or while she studies Dutch. Use established characters and roles: Sofia is a programmer; Karin is her team leader; Daan is her colleague; Marieke teaches Tuesday Dutch class. Do not invent a deadline, project detail, study result, or new schedule. Keep the present as the primary time frame.

**Grammar facts:**
- Recycle known *dat*-clauses after *vinden, denken, hopen,* and *zeggen*; briefly review the finite verb at the end.
- Introduce *omdat* for a reason and temporal *als* meaning “when” for a repeated or expected situation (e.g. *Als ik thuiswerk, …*). Do not teach hypothetical/conditional *als*; that is row 35.
- Teach verb-final order in all three subordinate clause types. When the subordinate clause comes first, the following main clause has inversion: *Omdat ik thuiswerk, drink ik thee.* Use commas consistently.
- Keep clauses short and avoid verb clusters or other advanced word-order patterns.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 genuinely useful new terms/chunks about work, study or reasons; check all earlier parts and cards. Do not use unsupported facts to create vocabulary.

**Practice and required features:** at least 7 practice blocks / 45 items, including choose *omdat/dat/als*, build subordinate clauses, repair word order, move a subordinate clause to the front, dialogue completion, translations both ways, and a reading with at least six comprehension questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, at least ten translated examples, grammar/important/warning callouts as appropriate, a nested list, numbered list, column list, 1–3 image ideas and no more than one link. Every item needs an answer and a useful note where errors or alternatives are likely.

**Off-limits:** conditional *als … dan* and *zou/zouden*; *want* contrast (row 31); relative clauses (33); purpose *om … te* (34); future grammar (36).

## Row 31 — Giving reasons

**Target:** distinguish coordinating *want* from subordinating *omdat* when giving a reason.

**Story and continuity:** Tuesday 12 January 2027, in a natural exchange connected to Sofia's work or Dutch class. This may reuse row 30's known activities, without adding a new work commitment or study schedule. The date fits the established Tuesday evening class; if Marieke/class is used, keep its 19.00–21.00 library setting.

**Grammar facts:**
- Both *want* and *omdat* introduce a reason and can connect two clauses, but *want* keeps main-clause V2 order while *omdat* sends the finite verb to the end.
- Show the same meaning with both orders where natural: *Ik neem de bus, want het regent.* / *Ik neem de bus omdat het regent.* A fronted *omdat*-clause triggers inversion in the main clause.
- Recycle commas and conjunction choice. Keep the meaning of *omdat* as “because”; don't add *daarom* as a new grammar target.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 new, useful words/chunks checked against Parts I–V and row 30. Don't count known conjunctions as new vocabulary.

**Practice and features:** at least 7 blocks / 45 items: identify clause order, choose *want/omdat* in context, combine clauses, repair word order, complete dialogue, translations both directions, and a reading with six or more comprehension questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, at least ten translated examples, nested and numbered lists, column list, required callouts, 1–3 image ideas and no more than one link. Give feedback notes for each item.

**Off-limits:** conditional *als* and *zou/zouden*; *als* temporal clause lesson beyond brief recycling of row 30; relative clauses, purpose infinitives and future grammar.

## Row 32 — Comparing things

**Target:** consolidate comparatives and introduce adjective/adverb superlatives, including common irregular forms.

**Story and continuity:** Wednesday 13 January 2027. Sofia and Noor compare familiar places, food or everyday activities. Reuse established facts (Utrecht/Valencia, familiar foods, swimming/skating, work/study) without inventing new visits, tastes, or preferences. Keep comparisons clear and friendly.

**Grammar facts:**
- Briefly review comparative *-er + dan*, *even/net zo/niet zo … als* from 18-b; avoid retelling all of row 18.
- Introduce regular superlative *-st/-ste*: adverbially/predicatively commonly *het* + form (*Utrecht is het rustigst*); before a noun use *de/het* + inflected *-ste* (*de rustigste straat, het rustigste café*). Keep form explanations clear and accurate.
- Teach common irregular pairs *goed → beter → best(e)* and *veel → meer → meest(e)*. Recycle *graag → liever → het liefst* only as already known from Part IV; note that it is a preference expression.
- Use *dan* for comparisons and preserve adjective spelling where needed. No relative clauses.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 new comparison adjectives/nouns/chunks that are genuinely useful and not already carded. Use familiar vocabulary for most example sentences.

**Practice and features:** at least 7 blocks / 45 items: form comparatives and superlatives, choose the natural degree, complete comparisons, correct *dan/als*, dialogue completion, translations both ways, and a reading with at least six questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, nested/numbered/column lists, appropriate callouts, 1–3 image ideas and no more than one link. Provide answer keys and notes.

**Off-limits:** relative clauses (33), purpose *om … te* (34), conditional *zou/zouden* (35), future forms (36), and unsupported opinions or story facts.

## Row 33 — People and things

**Target:** subject relative clauses with *die/dat* for identifying or describing people and things.

**Story and continuity:** Thursday 14 January 2027. Use familiar characters and objects in a natural exchange at work, at home, or in Dutch study. You may refer to previously established photos, a text/book, Sofia's apartment, or familiar places; do not invent a new person, object with backstory, or event. Do not add a new date to the main story beyond the lesson's day.

**Grammar facts:**
- *die* refers to a de-word or plural antecedent; *dat* refers to a singular het-word.
- In the relative clause, the finite verb goes at the end: *de collega die hier werkt*, *het boek dat op tafel ligt*.
- Keep the first lesson to subject relative pronouns. Object relatives, preposition + relative pronouns, and more complex pronoun patterns are out of scope.
- Recycle *dat* as a subordinating conjunction (row 24/30) only if the examples clearly distinguish it from relative *dat*.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 genuinely new terms/chunks checked against all prior lessons. Recycle familiar nouns and people where possible; don't give known nouns duplicate cards solely to practice *die/dat*.

**Practice and features:** at least 7 practice blocks / 45 items, including choose die/dat from antecedent gender/number, complete a short relative clause, combine two sentences, word order, dialogue completion, translation both ways, and a reading with six or more questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, relevant callouts, nested and numbered lists, a column list, 1–3 image ideas and no more than one link. Every item needs a key and note where useful.

**Off-limits:** object relative pronouns and prepositional relatives; relative clause grammar beyond subject die/dat; conditional or future grammar from rows 35–36.

## Row 34 — Explaining purpose

**Target:** *om … te + infinitive* for purpose, alongside a small set of common *te + infinitive* constructions.

**Story and continuity:** Friday 15 January 2027. Use Sofia's established work and Dutch study, familiar destinations (work, home, the library), or a simple familiar activity with Noor. Do not invent a new appointment, course schedule, deadline or project. Keep the purpose relation concrete and easy to see.

**Grammar facts:**
- Introduce *om … te + infinitive* to explain why someone does something: *Ik ga naar de bibliotheek om te studeren.* Place *te* before the infinitive; with separable verbs, it goes between prefix and verb (*op te ruimen*).
- Add a small set of frequent *te + infinitive* frames such as *proberen te …*, *vergeten te …*, or *het is fijn om … te …* only when needed and explained.
- Recycle the already-known *hoeven … te* and positional verb + *te* (22-b/23-b) as recognition or short review, not as new rules.
- Keep the infinitive at the end of its clause and avoid complex nested clauses.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 new, genuinely useful purpose/activity terms or chunks; check all prior ledgers and rows 30–33. Don't recard known infinitives unless the lesson introduces a new sense and notes it.

**Practice and features:** at least 7 blocks / 45 items: choose purpose vs reason, complete *om … te*, place *te* in separable verbs, sentence combining, dialogue completion, translations both ways, reading with six or more questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, nested and numbered lists, column list, appropriate callouts, 1–3 image ideas and no more than one link. Every item needs answer and feedback note where likely errors arise.

**Off-limits:** conditional *zou/zouden* and *als … dan* (row 35); future forms (36); complex verb clusters or nested subordinate clauses.

## Row 35 — Conditions and possibilities

**Target:** hypothetical *als … dan* clauses and conditional *zou/zouden* with an infinitive.

**Story and continuity:** Monday 18 January 2027. Sofia and Noor discuss hypothetical choices about established places and activities (Utrecht/Valencia, work/study, swimming/skating, familiar food) without declaring a new real plan or preference. Make clear the situations are imagined, not new events in the timeline.

**Grammar facts:**
- Teach hypothetical *als* (“if”) with the finite verb at the end of the subordinate clause; distinguish it from temporal *als* (“when”) in row 30.
- Teach *zou* for singular and *zouden* for plural/polite forms, followed by an infinitive at the end: *Als ik tijd had, zou ik …* / *Als we tijd hadden, zouden we …*.
- Show *dan* as optional/frequent in the main clause after the condition, with main-clause V2: *Als het mooi weer was, (dan) zou ik gaan zwemmen.*
- Keep a few clear patterns; do not teach an exhaustive conditional taxonomy, counterfactual past, or future choices.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 new useful hypothetical/choice vocabulary, checked against all previous parts and rows 30–34. Do not create cards for known conditional words unless a genuinely new sense is taught.

**Practice and features:** at least 7 blocks / 45 items, including distinguish temporal vs hypothetical *als*, complete *zou/zouden*, order the clauses, transform a simple preference into a hypothetical, dialogue completion, translations both ways, and a reading with at least six questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, ten translated examples, nested and numbered lists, a column list, appropriate callouts, 1–3 image ideas and no more than one link. Supply answer keys and useful notes.

**Off-limits:** future forms (row 36); advanced counterfactual past; unsupported real plans, preferences, or events; object relative clauses or advanced subordinate structures.

**Acceptance checks for rows 33–35:** story facts agree with [storyline.md](storyline.md); terms are unique; Dutch forms and answers are natural; row boundaries are maintained; each independent reviewer records and resolves issues; validator prints OK.

## Row 36 — Future plans

**Target:** choose among present tense with a future time expression, *gaan + infinitive*, and *zullen + infinitive* for near-future plans, intentions, expectations, promises and offers.

**Story and continuity:** Tuesday 19 January 2027, at Marieke's established 19.00 Dutch class in the library. Sofia and Piotr talk about ordinary next-day plans and the class timetable. The regular Tuesday class time is already established. Do not invent work shifts, travel, a deadline, or a new appointment. Any new intention stated by a character should be clearly a plan, not an event that has already happened.

**Grammar facts:**
- Recycle present tense + future time expression for a settled arrangement or timetable (*De les begint morgen om …* only when the context supports a fixed time).
- Teach *gaan + infinitive* for an intention or plan (*Ik ga morgen mijn woorden herhalen*).
- Teach *zullen + infinitive* for a prediction/expectation and for a promise or spontaneous offer. Recycle suggestion/offer questions from 21-a (*Zullen we …? Zal ik …?*) without retelling that lesson. Contrast meaning in context; avoid claiming rigid rules where Dutch usage overlaps.
- Keep time expressions clear and clauses short. Recycle future meanings only; don't add another future construction or conditional patterns beyond 35.

**Vocabulary:** 15–35 genuinely new everyday planning/time terms, checked against all prior parts and lessons 30–35. Don't card known future time words or *gaan/zullen* as new vocabulary.

**Practice and features:** at least 7 practice blocks / 45 items: choose the natural future form by context, complete *gaan/zullen*, identify fixed arrangement vs intention vs expectation/offer, reorder clauses, complete a dialogue, translate both ways, and read a short plan/timetable with at least six comprehension questions. Include two 6–12 turn dialogues, at least ten translated examples, nested and numbered lists, a column list, suitable callouts, 1–3 image ideas, and no more than one link. Every item needs an answer and useful feedback notes.

**Off-limits:** future perfect; exhaustive claims about all uses of *zullen*; new conditional grammar beyond row 35; unsupported story commitments.

**Acceptance checks for row 36:** storyline continuity; unique vocabulary; natural Dutch and answer keys; future-form distinctions accurately explained; independent review fixes resolved; validator prints OK.
