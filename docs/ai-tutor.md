# AI tutor

Status: approved product change (C14, 2026-10-15), in progress. C14.0 (docs and decisions), the provider layer (C14f), the tutor API (C14g), and the web (C14i) are implemented. Study plans, the session panel, the context scope, dated notes, topics, tutor words, observations, personal practices, and the reading component are C14l, owned by [tutor sessions and memory](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md). The delivery steps are in [AI_PLAN.md](../AI_PLAN.md) and the phases in the [roadmap](roadmap.md#course-phases).

The tutor is a chat assistant that teaches from the library's lessons and the learner's own learning data. It can find the learner's next lesson and teach it, ask practice questions through answer components in the chat, record what the learner got right and wrong, and, with the learner's confirmation, finish or reset lessons and save practices for other learners. This document owns the tutor: its entry point, conversations, formatting, tools, confirmation, components, providers, and limits. The learning data it reads and writes is owned elsewhere: [word memory](words.md#word-memory) and [stored pronunciation scores](words.md#pronunciation-assessment) by words, [practice attempts](courses.md#practice-attempts), [lesson states](courses.md#lesson-states), [reset](courses.md#reset), and [AI practices](courses.md#ai-practices) by courses, and [on-demand audio](speech.md#on-demand-audio) by speech. Study plans, the context scope, notes, topics, tutor words, and personal practices (C14l) are owned by [tutor sessions and memory](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md).

## Entry point

- The tutor lives at `/ai`. It is the Tutor item in the navigation rail (since the Lumen shell, 2026-10-16), and a call to action on `/courses` (Ask your tutor) also opens it with a new conversation, which is created when the learner sends the first message. An open conversation is `/ai?conversation=<id>`. `/ai?prompt=<text>` opens a new conversation with its composer prefilled (at most 4,000 characters; ignored with `conversation`); nothing is sent until the learner sends it. The lesson page's [lesson box](courses.md#lesson-box) uses it for Ask AI (talk the lesson's grammar and topics through) and Ask for more practices like this (Lumen, 2026-10-16).
- The tutor is the one exception to [desktop first](what_is_it.md#desktop-first) (approved product change, 2026-10-15): `/ai` and every tutor component work on phones and desktops. On wide screens recent conversations continue the app rail directly under Tutor, the chat keeps the centred conversation column, and a right rail shows the current conversation (with when it was last active, relative to now, such as "3 hours ago"; the exact time is its tooltip), recent tool activity described by each call's [summary](#tools), progress counts, and the session summary. The page is exactly the window's height (changed 2026-10-16): the chat header and the composer or question dock stay put and only the messages scroll, while the rails scroll on their own. Below the app-rail breakpoint the Conversations action opens the ordinary navigation drawer; the right rail is omitted so the chat remains one column.
- Every signed-in account that has completed setup can use it. There is no per-user opt-in; the page says plainly, above the first message of a new conversation, that conversations are processed by external AI providers that may keep them (see [security and privacy](security-and-privacy.md#ai-tutor)), and nothing is sent until the learner writes. The full notice shows on the account's first visit in a browser; after that it is one line ("Processed by external AI providers") that opens to the full notice, remembered in that browser only (changed 2026-10-16).

## Conversations

- A conversation belongs to one account and is never shown to anyone else.
- The conversation list shows active conversations, most recently updated first, directly under Tutor in the app rail. **Include archived**, at the bottom of the list, adds archived conversations into the same recency-sorted list. New conversation starts an empty one; its title is the first user message, cut to 80 characters, until it is renamed (see [conversation titles](#conversation-titles)). A long title uses the course sub-item type and stays on one line, fading at the rail edge. Each row also shows its last-updated time relative to now (with the exact local date and time in its tooltip), the conversation's token total (tokens in and out of every model request made for it), and a small sparkle before a title the tutor chose.
- Conversations are stored permanently. Each row's three-dots menu offers Show summary, Change title, and **Archive** or **Unarchive**; summary and title open the shared Conversation details dialog. There is no delete. An archived conversation can be read but not continued (no new message and no answer to an open component or card) until it is unarchived. Archiving keeps the conversation's last-updated time, so its order does not change.
- Messages load newest page first, by cursor; Show earlier messages at the top of the chat loads the next older page.
- **Model.** The chat header has a model picker: Default, then each enabled provider's configured model (see [providers and fallback](#providers-and-fallback)). A new conversation keeps the choice until its first message creates it. Changing the model applies from the next turn; earlier messages keep the model that wrote them. The picker is hidden when there is only one model to choose from. Closed, it shows the short model name (without the vendor path or a `:free` tag, so `openai/gpt-oss-120b:free` reads `gpt-oss-120b`), followed by "· Default" for the default; the open list names Default or the provider above each short name, with the full model ID as a tooltip.
- A turn that fails partway (a provider error after fallback, or a refused tool) ends with an error part and a Try again action that resends the last user message. Tool results already written stay written.

### Conversation titles

Added 2026-10-16; placement changed with the Lumen tutor page on the same date and the row menu was added 2026-10-17. Both the learner and the tutor can rename a conversation; each title records who set it (`auto`, `tutor`, or `user`). The header's three-dots Conversation details action and the row's Show summary and Change title actions open one dialog with the title field (trimmed, whitespace collapsed, 1–80 characters), the current session summary, token counts, progress counts, and recent tool-call summary; archived conversations can be renamed too. The tutor names a conversation with `set_conversation_title` once its topic is clear, and may rename its own title when the topic clearly changes; once the learner has set a title, the tutor's call is refused with `TITLE_SET_BY_LEARNER` and the first message no longer replaces it. A title the tutor chose carries a small sparkle in the header. Renaming keeps the conversation's last-updated time. The header and list refresh after every turn, so a new conversation shows its title as soon as its first message is stored.

### Conversation details and session context

Added 2026-10-16; moved into Conversation details with the Lumen tutor page on the same date. The dialog shows the learner's own usage: today's (UTC) tokens across all their conversations (in and out, from the `ai_usage` rows that carry a conversation; AI practices on the lesson page and other accounts are left out), the open conversation's tokens over its whole life, and today's turns of the [daily cap](#usage-and-limits). It refreshes after every turn and shows counts only, never cost. It also derives learner turns, completed answer components, and up to four recent visible tool results from the messages already returned to the web; this is presentation of stored conversation data, not a second memory or analytics store.

On wide screens the right rail repeats the lightweight session evidence that is useful while chatting: the conversation title and last-active date, learner-turn and completed-activity counts, and up to four recent visible tool results. Its final card is temporarily labelled **This session so far** (the prototype's “Try asking” position) and shows an excerpt of the latest tutor response, or the opening learner request before a response exists. This is a deterministic excerpt, not a new model-generated summary. The right rail is omitted on narrower layouts.

## Messages and formatting

- **User messages** are plain text, at most 4,000 characters, shown with line breaks preserved and only safe `http`/`https` URLs linked, like journal posts.
- **Assistant messages** are a sequence of parts shown together in one Tutor · AI turn card: text, tool steps, components, and confirmation cards. Text parts may use a small Markdown subset: **bold**, *italic*, bulleted and numbered lists (one level), inline code, short headings (one level, rendered at card-title size), and `http`/`https` links. Numbered lists keep list semantics and use round step counters. A successful visible tool result that already carries a lesson title may show that stored lesson and course title as a source chip; the chip adds no attachment, route, or reference behavior. A long answer is split into several small sections under those headings. Anything else (raw HTML, images, tables, code blocks, other link schemes) is shown as its literal text. The renderer is Wordinator's own and never injects HTML.
- **Target-language spans.** The model marks Dutch or German text it wants spoken as `[[...]]`. When a text part is stored, the server parses the marks out (the contracts' `parseTargetSpans`) into the part's `spans`, offsets into the text without the marks; an unclosed or empty mark stays literal text. Each span renders as normal text with a speaker button that requests [on-demand audio](speech.md#on-demand-audio); spans are numbered in order across the message's text parts. The marking is stored with the message; the web cannot add spans. Streamed text deltas still carry the raw marks, so the web parses the text it has so far and replaces it with the stored message when the turn ends.
- Assistant text streams in as it is generated. A stopped stream keeps what arrived.
- This subset is the only rich text outside course lessons; see [product requirements](product-requirements.md#global-content-rules).

## Learner context

Each turn starts with a compact snapshot of the learner in the system prompt: their [tutor profile](#tutor-profile) and tutor notes, up to five recently completed lessons and five lessons in progress (with their storyline share), their eight weakest words from [word memory](words.md#word-memory), their five most recent practice misses (no match and no acceptable verdict), and the readable courses in library order (number, ID, title, and lessons completed of published; at most 20), so "the second course" needs no lookup. The snapshot is JSON placed after the instructions and labelled as data. The instructions also tell the model to talk to the learner as "you", to mark a whole target-language sentence once, to label no parts of a reply (no Explanation or Request headings), and to call tools only through function calling: a tool name or call syntax written in the text does nothing, since the renderer shows it as literal text, and every practice question goes through an `ask_*` tool. They tell it to practise in [sets](#practice-sets), to find a named lesson with `list_lessons` instead of building an ID, and never to repeat a call whose result it already has. With it the model receives the conversation's latest 40 stored messages. Everything else is fetched through tools.

Since C14l the learner chooses the snapshot's sections and sizes per conversation (the [context scope](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#context-scope)); the snapshot then also carries the current time, the turns left today, the active [plan](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#study-plans), dated [notes](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#tutor-notes) with due follow-ups, recent [topics](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#practice-topics), [tutor words](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#tutor-words), and earlier plans, and names everything with [short references](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#short-references) instead of UUIDs.

The tutor never sees journal posts, comments, reactions, notifications, other learners' data, email, or credentials. Sending journal content to the tutor is a possible later addition and needs its own product decision. What each provider receives is listed in [security and privacy](security-and-privacy.md#ai-tutor).

## Tools

Every tool runs in the API as the signed-in account, under the same visibility rules as the matching read: lessons the learner cannot read are invisible, and no tool takes a user ID. The tutor reads only **published** lessons of courses the account can see in its library (an archived course stays readable to those who can see it, but its progress never changes); an unpublished lesson, a draft course the account cannot see, and another library's lesson all answer `LESSON_NOT_FOUND`, exactly like a missing one.

- **Read tools:** `search_words` (by term, meaning, or form), `get_weak_words`, `get_recent_words`, `get_course_progress` (without a course: the readable courses with lessons completed; with one: each lesson's state, storyline share, practices, and words, as the [Progress tab](courses.md#lesson-progress-tab) shows them), `list_lessons` (the readable published lessons in library order, each with its ID, number in its course, title, state, and course with its number; filtered by `courseId` or by a `query` matched case-insensitively against lesson and course titles; at most 100), `get_lesson` (by ID, or `next`; blocks with their IDs, new words with their IDs, examples, dialogues, and practices with their authors' versions, never item notes), `get_recent_mistakes`, and `list_ai_practices`. A `LESSON_NOT_FOUND` result tells the model to find the ID with `list_lessons`.
- **Interactive tools** (`ask_fill_in`, `ask_translate`, `ask_word_order`, `ask_article`, `ask_choice`, `ask_quick_review`, `ask_say_it`, `ask_dictation`, `ask_fix_mistake`) show an [answer component](#answer-components) and wait for the learner's answer.
- **Writes that need confirmation:** `complete_lesson`, `reset_lesson`, and `save_ai_practice`. Since C15 `save_ai_practice` also takes the practice's focus (`grammar`, `vocabulary`, `reading`, `listening`, or `writing`), which [learning time](learning-time.md#what-is-measured) uses to categorise time spent in it; the confirmation card names it.
- Translate, fill in, word order, fix the mistake, and dictation take [word hints](#word-hints).
- **Writes without confirmation:** `record_word_review`, only for a word that an answered component of this conversation tested, `update_tutor_notes`, the tutor's own notes about the learner (replacing the previous notes), and `set_conversation_title`, which renames the current conversation unless the learner named it (see [conversation titles](#conversation-titles)). A title is the conversation's own label, not progress or shared content.
- **C14l** ([tutor sessions and memory](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md)) adds, all without confirmation since they change only the learner's private study data: `start_plan`, `update_plan`, `finish_plan`, and `get_plan_results`; `add_note`, `edit_note`, and `remove_note` (replacing `update_tutor_notes`); `get_recent_topics`; `add_tutor_words`, `update_tutor_word`, and `get_tutor_words`; `observe_words`; `save_personal_practice`; and the interactive `ask_reading`. Every `ask_*` call may carry a `topic`.

A tool with invalid arguments, an unknown name, or an ID the account cannot read comes back to the model as a failed result with an error code it can correct; the turn carries on. When the model asks for several tools at once they run in order. One response shows either a [practice set](#practice-sets) of up to six components or one confirmation card: a card after a component, anything after a card, and a seventh component are skipped (`SKIPPED`) and the model is told to ask again later. Read tools still run alongside a set. A read tool called again with the same arguments in the same turn is not run again; it answers `REPEATED_CALL` and the model is told to use the first result.

**Visible steps.** Every read and write tool (not the `ask_*` questions or the confirmed writes, whose components and cards describe themselves) requires a `summary` argument (added 2026-10-16): one line for the learner, in English, saying what the call does, such as "Reading lesson 3: Boodschappen" (whitespace collapsed, 1–120 characters). It is validated before the tool's own arguments, so a call without one fails with `INVALID_ARGUMENTS` and the model can correct it; it is stored in the result's `detail` as `summary`, shown as the step's What it did detail and as the call's line in the session rail and Conversation details (results stored before the change fall back to the tool's plain-words label), and ignored when matching a repeated read. Every tool call shows in the chat as a compact step chip in plain words (Looked up lesson 6, Checked your weak words, Updated progress), with an expandable detail of what was read or changed. Consecutive steps fold into one chip (Used 4 tools, with the count of failed steps), closed by default, that opens to the single chips; a single step stays a plain chip. A failed tool shows as a failed step with its own label (Couldn't open that lesson), never a done label with empty titles. Steps are stored with the message: a `tool_call` part (the tool's name and arguments; the arguments of an `ask_*` call are left out of every response, since they hold the reference answer) and a `tool_result` part with `ok`, an error code, and a small `detail` of plain values (counts, titles, IDs) for the chip. The data the model received is stored with the result but never returned to the web.

## Confirmation

Tools that change progress or create content stop the turn and show a confirmation card in the chat: what will happen, in plain words, with **Confirm** and **Cancel**.

- Confirmed writes are finishing a lesson, [resetting a lesson](courses.md#reset) (the card names everything that will be deleted: the saved position, the completion, and the counts of practice progress rows, attempts, word reviews, pronunciation scores, and bookmarks at the time of asking), and [saving an AI practice](courses.md#ai-practices) (the card shows the practice's view and the lessons it is tagged to). An archived course is refused before a card is shown.
- Confirm runs the write and resumes the turn; Cancel resumes it with the refusal, and the tutor carries on without the change. A card left unanswered stays open; it is answered at most once. The lesson is resolved again when the card is confirmed, so a lesson that became unreadable or archived meanwhile is refused, and the card stores the outcome (`ok` and an error code). A confirmed save checks every anchor against the published document again and is refused with `ANCHOR_NOT_FOUND` when one is missing (C14h).
- Reviews and attempts that come from the learner's own answers to components are recorded without a card. Writing them is what answering means.
- Every progress change the tutor makes writes a `progress_events` audit row with actor `agent` (see [data model](data-model.md#progress_events)). There is no undo; a reset is reversed only by studying again.

## Practice sets

The tutor practises in sets rather than single questions (approved product change, 2026-10-16). In one response it asks 3 to 5 questions (at most six) with `ask_*` tools, mixing kinds and covering different words or points, and the learner answers them all before the tutor replies.

- **Question dock** (changed 2026-10-18). While the last assistant message has open questions (a set or a single one), they leave the message, which shows one muted line ("Answer the 3 questions below"), and the composer under the messages is replaced by the question dock. It shows one question at a time under "Question N of M · checked together" and a row of step pips (answered, skipped, not answered; each one jumps to its question). Answers are drafts until the set is sent: Previous and the pips go back to a question with its answer kept and editable, and the questions stay mounted while hidden, so typed text, picks, and opened hints survive. The footer has Previous at the start and Skip and Next at the end; on the last question Next becomes **Check answers** (just **Check** for a single question). Picking an option or pressing Enter in a typed answer saves it and moves to the next question; a quick review or a spoken answer stays so its result can be read. Questions left unanswered when Check answers is pressed go as skips. On the last question, when no question in the set has an answer, **Skip** becomes **Skip all**. That opens the regular text composer with a prompt asking what the learner wanted to practise instead. Submitting the text records it as a failed `ask_*` tool result for each skipped question and then resumes the tutor with the learner's text; the tutor turn does not start until the text is submitted. A single question's Skip sends at once. The dock scrolls inside itself (at most 70% of the chat's height, and `44rem`) so its footer and some of the conversation stay in view. A minimize toggle at the end of its head (added 2026-10-16) folds it to the head alone (position, pips, and the toggle; a single question's head reads "Your tutor's question") so earlier messages get the chat's height; the questions stay mounted, so answers, typed text, and opened hints survive, and the toggle or any pip brings them back. An archived conversation shows its open questions in place, read-only.
- **Sending the set.** Check answers sends every open question of the message in one request (`POST .../messages/:messageId/answers`). Every answer is graded before any is stored, so one that does not fit its question changes nothing; then all are stored and recorded, their results show in place, and the turn resumes once with every grade. It counts as one turn. The single-question route (`.../tool-calls/:toolCallId/respond`) still answers confirmation cards and older clients.
- The last answer or skip of the set resumes the turn: every grade of the set reaches the model together, and the tutor corrects the set in one reply (what went well, each mistake with the right form, and the next set). All the set's results then show in place. Only that last answer is held to the [daily turn cap](#usage-and-limits).
- Answers are stored part by part, so answers sent at once never overwrite each other, and exactly one request resumes the turn.
- A single question is still asked when the learner asks for one. Confirmation cards are never shown with a set.

## Word hints

Questions the learner has to produce an answer for can carry word hints (approved product change, 2026-10-16): translate, fill in the blank, word order, fix the mistake, and dictation. Multiple choice, de or het, quick review, and Say it have none, since a hint would give the answer away.

- **Source.** A hint is a lesson word from the published index plus a cue: the words of the question it helps with ("tidy up", "in the morning"). The model sends only references, `hints: [{ lessonId, wordId, cue }]` (at most four), and is told to always give a translation hints. When the component is created the server resolves each word under the learner's read rules, like `words`, and stores its term, meaning, and forms in the view; a hint word the learner cannot read refuses the call with `WORD_NOT_FOUND`. The model never writes a hint's target-language text. A saved [AI practice](courses.md#ai-practices) resolves its hints again on each lesson-page read, for the reader.
- **Hidden until opened.** The question shows a Word hints row with only the cues. Opening one shows its word and forms in place, with no request and no turn, since the word is already in the view. Hiding them keeps the question useful; it is not authorization (see [concealment](security-and-privacy.md)).
- **Answering.** The answer carries the opened hints as word references (`hintsUsed`), which must be the component's own (otherwise `ANSWER_INVALID`). They never change the grade. The attempt stores how many were opened (`hints_used`), and a word the component tests whose hint was opened is reviewed as Again even when the answer is right, since the learner did not produce it unaided. The model receives the opened hints' cues (`hintsOpened`) with the grade, so it can practise those words again. Once answered, every hint shows, the opened ones marked.

## Answer components

Interactive tools render answer components in the chat. Each component's payload and its answer have a contracts schema, and the server grades every answer, so the model never decides a deterministic result.

| Component | What the learner does | Graded by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fill in the blank | types the missing word or words into each blank | server, with the shared answer matching |
| Translate into Dutch | types or says a translation of an English sentence | server comparison with the reference, then the model's verdict |
| Word order | puts shuffled chunks in order by clicking or dragging (keyboard accessible) | server |
| De or het | picks the article for one or more nouns with two large toggle buttons each | server |
| Multiple choice | picks one of 3–4 options for meaning, grammar, or form (keys `1`–`4`) | server |
| Quick review | drill-style cards for a handful of words: Show answer, then Again or Got it | self-graded, like [Drill](words.md#drill) |
| Say it | hears the tutor's sentence and repeats it | Microsoft [pronunciation assessment](words.md#pronunciation-assessment) |
| Dictation | hears audio only and types what they heard | server, word by word |
| Fix the mistake | rewrites a sentence that has one error | server comparison with the reference, then the model's verdict |
| Reading (C14l) | reads a short passage and answers 1–5 choice or true/false questions | server; see [reading component](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#reading-component) |

- **Target-language marks.** The model may mark target-language text with `[[...]]` in a component's display fields (a multiple-choice question and its options, a source sentence, a translation, a hint); the web shows each mark as an emphasised span, never as brackets. Every other text field (answers, references, chunks, nouns, Say it and Dictation sentences, a fill-in prompt) is stored without marks, so grading and audio see plain text.
- **Payloads.** The model sends a component's full payload, reference answer included, as the `ask_*` tool's arguments; the contracts (`@wordinator/contracts/tutor`) validate it. Every payload may name the lesson it practises (`lessonId`) and up to eight lesson words it tests (`words`, lesson and word IDs, which must be in the published index of a lesson the learner can read). The web receives only the component's **view**, which leaves out the reference: a fill-in's answers, the correct option, the articles, word order's solved order (its chunks are shuffled once, when the component is created), and a dictation's sentence (audio only). Quick review cards carry their words' term, meaning, forms, and example, read by the server. De or het offers *de* and *het*, or *der*, *die*, and *das* for German nouns.
- **Deterministic kinds** (fill in, word order, de or het, multiple choice, dictation) are graded by the server alone, with `blankMatches` and `normalizeAnswer` from the contracts where text is compared. Dictation compares normalised words by position, and extra words count against. Quick review is self-graded (correct when every card is Got it), and Say it passes from an overall pronunciation score of 60.
- **Open kinds** (translate, fix the mistake) are first compared with the reference. A match is accepted at once; otherwise the model judges the answer as `acceptable` or `error` with a short note (addressed to the learner as "you" and naming the concrete word, form, or order to change), since a sentence can be right in several ways. The verdict and note are stored on the [attempt](courses.md#practice-attempts). The judge is one extra model request without tools; if it fails or answers nonsense, the answer is stored without a verdict and not accepted.
- **Recording.** Each graded answer writes a practice attempt with source `ai_chat`, practice kind `ai`, the tool call ID as its practice ID, and the conversation, under the component's lesson (or its first tested word's lesson). A component tied to no lesson writes no attempt, since attempts belong to a course; its answer and grade stay in the conversation. Since C14l a component tied to no lesson still writes its attempt, without a course or lesson, when it carries a [topic](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#practice-topics) or tests [tutor words](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#tutor-words), and tutor words get reviews like lesson words; every attempt and review stores the component's topic. For every lesson word it tests the answer writes a [word review](words.md#word-memory) with source `ai_chat`: Got it when the grade is correct and Again otherwise, in the produce direction (understand for multiple choice), and for quick review each card's own result and direction. Say it writes a [pronunciation attempt](words.md#pronunciation-assessment) (surface `ai_chat`, with its first tested word) and no review or practice attempt. Nothing is recorded for a word or lesson of an archived course. The grade then goes back to the model so it can continue.
- **Speech.** Translate into Dutch has a microphone that uses the learner's chosen [recognizer](words.md#recognizers), with a longer answer window for sentences. Say it and Dictation play their sentence through [on-demand audio](speech.md#on-demand-audio). Say it needs the Microsoft recognizer; with the browser recognizer it says so and offers to switch this device to Microsoft in place (the same preference as in Settings); it can also be skipped there, like any waiting component.
- A component is answered once (a failed send re-opens it). After answering it shows the result in place (the same verdict badge as drill cards: Matches!, Not quite, or the tutor's note) and no longer accepts input.
- **Skip** (C14h). A waiting component blocks the composer until it is answered, so every one offers a quiet Skip in the chat. Skipping is the component's one answer: nothing is graded or recorded (no attempt, review, or pronunciation attempt, and its words do not count as tested for `record_word_review`), the model is told the learner skipped it, and the turn resumes as after an answer. The component then collapses to a muted Skipped line. The lesson page's [AI practices](courses.md#ai-practices) have no Skip; they never block anything.
- Missed lessons: now and then the tutor mixes words from [missed lessons](courses.md#lesson-states) into a component to gauge whether the learner already knows them.

Later, if useful after release: a verb conjugation grid (present, perfectum with *hebben* or *zijn*, imperfectum), matching pairs (term and meaning), and role-play turns where the tutor plays a character from the course's dialogues and the learner answers by text or voice with optional hint chips.

## Next lesson

Asked what to study, the tutor calls `get_lesson` with `next`: the next lesson in the course the learner was most recently active in (a saved position, a completion, or a practice attempt; archived courses are skipped, and without any activity the first readable course), chosen like the course page's [progress card](courses.md#lesson-player) (the most recently moved unfinished lesson, otherwise the first one not completed). It reads the published document and can teach it in the chat: explain its grammar, introduce its words, read its dialogue, and ask questions about it with components. When the learner is done, the tutor offers to mark the lesson complete through a confirmation card; confirming records the completion exactly as finishing the lesson does.

## Tutor profile

Settings has a Tutor section ([settings and administration](settings-and-administration.md#tutor-settings)) with:

- **Native language** (default English): the language the tutor explains in when the learner has not said otherwise. One of a fixed list of ISO codes in the contracts (`TUTOR_NATIVE_LANGUAGES`: en, nl, de, fr, es, it, pt, pl, tr, ru, uk, ar, fa, zh, ja, hi).
- **Reply language** (`native`, `target` for the library language, or `mixed`; default `native`): how much of its own text the tutor writes in the target language.
- **Goals**: optional plain text, at most 500 characters, for what the learner wants to work on.

The tutor keeps its own **tutor notes** about the learner (plain text, at most 2,000 characters) through `update_tutor_notes`, for example recurring mistakes. The learner can read and clear them in the Tutor section. They are part of the snapshot. C14l replaces them with dated entries with follow-up dates ([tutor notes](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#tutor-notes)), and the profile gains the learner's default [context scope](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#context-scope).

## Providers and fallback

- The API calls models through one OpenAI-compatible chat-completions client with three configured targets: **OpenRouter**, **Cloudflare Workers AI** (its OpenAI-compatible endpoint through the `AI` binding), and **Microsoft Azure AI Foundry**. Anthropic models and agents are not used.
- The default order is Azure (`gpt-4.1-mini`), then OpenRouter's free `openai/gpt-oss-120b:free`, then Workers AI (`@cf/openai/gpt-oss-120b`) (changed 2026-10-16: Azure calls tools most reliably, the free OpenRouter model is rate-limited, and Llama 4 Scout on Workers AI wrote `ask_*` calls as text instead of calling the tool). The defaults apply only while no AI settings are stored; stored settings keep their order and models until the superuser changes them. A `429`, a `5xx`, a refused key (`401`/`403`), a `404` (a model or deployment the provider no longer serves, such as a retired OpenRouter slug or a missing Azure deployment), a network failure, or a timeout moves the turn to the next target, as long as that target has not streamed any text yet; an invalid request does not, and neither does a failure after text has been streamed. A target turned off, or without its key or binding, is skipped. Only models that support tool calling may be configured. GPT-5-family and o-series models (matched by model ID, including vendor paths such as `openai/gpt-5-mini`) are sent `max_completion_tokens` and no `temperature` on the HTTP targets, because they reject `max_tokens` and a non-default temperature with a `400`.
- The superuser sets the order and the model per target in the [AI settings](settings-and-administration.md#ai-settings), on the [AI providers and models page](settings-and-administration.md#ai-providers-and-models), which also tests each connection without metering it. Each assistant message stores the provider and model that wrote it.
- **Per-conversation model** (added 2026-10-16). A learner can choose one of the enabled targets that has its key or binding for a conversation. That target's configured model goes first for every model request of the conversation's turns, including the open-answer judge, and the other targets follow in the configured order as fallback. Default (no choice) follows the configured order. A chosen target that the superuser later turns off is ignored until it is turned back on. Learners pick a target, never a model ID; the superuser still sets which model each target uses. The lesson page's [AI practices](courses.md#ai-practices) judge has no conversation and always uses the configured order.
- OpenRouter's free models may log prompts at the model host; see [security and privacy](security-and-privacy.md#ai-tutor).

## Usage and limits

- **Daily turns.** Each account has at most 40 tutor turns per UTC day (one user message or one component or confirmation answer that resumes the model counts as a turn; the earlier answers of a [practice set](#practice-sets) do not resume it and do not count). The superuser can change the cap. The API checks it before the first model call and refuses with `429 AI_DAILY_LIMIT` and `resetsAt` (the next UTC midnight); the composer then says when the limit resets and stays disabled until then. A turn is counted by the `ai_usage` row of its first model request (marked as a turn start), whether or not the request succeeded; fallback attempts and the further model calls of the turn's tool loop are not counted again, and a turn that reached no provider is not counted. The judge request of an open answer is metered but never marked as a turn start; the resumed loop's first request counts the turn.
- **Per turn.** A turn runs at most 8 tool calls besides its `ask_*` questions, which do not count, so a set can follow the reads that prepared it; after that the model is called once more without tools, so it answers in text, and further tool calls in one response are refused with `TOOL_LIMIT`. No model request starts after 100 seconds; the turn then ends with a `TURN_LIMIT` error part. Each model request asks for at most 2,000 output tokens; without a cap Workers AI stops at 256, which cuts off answers and tool arguments.
- **Speech.** On-demand audio has its own daily character cap per account; see [on-demand audio](speech.md#on-demand-audio).
- **Metering.** Every model request writes an `ai_usage` row (provider, model, tokens in and out, cost, success), and every on-demand speech request a `speech_usage` row. The superuser sees both on the [analytics page](settings-and-administration.md#analytics).

## Prompt injection and scope

- Lesson text, words, the learner's answers, and tool results reach the model as quoted data in tool results, never as instructions.
- Tools take only IDs of things the account can read and never another user's ID; the API scopes every call to the signed-in account regardless of what the model asks for.
- Every change to progress or shared content goes through a [confirmation](#confirmation) card that the learner answers in the web, so text in a lesson cannot finish, reset, or publish anything on its own.
- `record_word_review` accepts only words that a component in the same conversation tested, so the model cannot write reviews for arbitrary words.
- On-demand speech reads text only from stored assistant messages, never from the request.

## API

Routes sit under `/api/ai/...`, after authentication and completed setup. Every conversation, message, and tool call ID is resolved under the signed-in account; another account's IDs return `404`.

Shapes are in the contracts' `@wordinator/contracts/tutor` subpath.

- `GET /api/ai/conversations?archived=true|false&cursor=` lists conversations, 20 a page, most recently updated first (`tutorConversationPageSchema`); `POST /api/ai/conversations` creates an empty one (`201`, title empty until the first message); `GET .../conversations/:id` reads one; `POST .../conversations/:id/archive` and `.../unarchive` move it (idempotent); `PATCH .../conversations/:id` with `{ provider }` (a provider, or `null` for Default) sets its [model](#providers-and-fallback) and keeps its last-updated time, answering `409 AI_PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE` for a provider that is off or has no key or binding. Each answers `{ conversation }`, which carries `provider` (`null` for Default).
- `PATCH .../conversations/:id` also takes `{ title }` (alone or with `provider`; at least one is required): the learner's title, trimmed with whitespace collapsed to one line, 1–80 characters, stored with `titleSource: "user"`. Every conversation response carries `titleSource` (`auto`, `tutor`, or `user`).
- `GET /api/ai/usage?conversationId=` answers the learner's own [conversation details](#conversation-details-and-session-context) (`tutorUsageResponseSchema`): `today` with `tokensIn`, `tokensOut`, `turns`, `turnCap`, and `resetsAt`, and `conversation` with that conversation's `tokensIn` and `tokensOut` (`null` without `conversationId`). Another account's conversation is `404 CONVERSATION_NOT_FOUND`; an invalid ID is `400`.
- `GET /api/ai/models` lists the models a conversation can use: the enabled targets that have their key or binding, in the configured order, as `{ items: [{ provider, model }] }` (`tutorModelsResponseSchema`). The first is the Default.
- `GET .../conversations/:id/messages?cursor=` reads messages newest first, 30 a page, by `(created_at, id)` cursor (`tutorMessagePageSchema`). A message has its role, status (`complete`, `awaiting_user`, or `error`), parts, and the provider and model that wrote it.
- `POST .../conversations/:id/messages` with `{ text }` (plain text, trimmed, at most 4,000 characters) starts a turn and streams it as server-sent events. Errors come before the stream as JSON: `409 CONVERSATION_ARCHIVED` for an archived conversation, `409 TURN_IN_PROGRESS` while a component or confirmation waits, `400` for an invalid body, and `429 AI_DAILY_LIMIT` at the cap (nothing is stored then).
- `POST .../messages/:messageId/answers` with `{ answers: [{ toolCallId, answer } | { toolCallId, skip: true }] }` (`tutorSetAnswersRequestSchema`, 1–10, each tool call once) answers every open question of the message together and streams like a respond: one `answered` event per question, then the resumed turn (or `done` while a confirmation card still waits). It answers `400 ANSWERS_INCOMPLETE` when an open question is missing, `400 ANSWER_INVALID` when an answer does not fit its question (nothing is stored), `404 TOOL_CALL_NOT_FOUND` for a question not in the message, `409 TOOL_CALL_ANSWERED` once answered, and the daily cap and archived refusals as a new message does.
- `POST .../messages/:messageId/skip-set` with `{ reason }` skips an entirely unanswered practice set and adds the reason as a user message before resuming. The failed result for each original `ask_*` tool call carries that reason as `LEARNER_DECLINED_PRACTICE`; the tutor can respond to the user's alternative request without grading or recording answers.
- `POST .../tool-calls/:toolCallId/respond` answers a component (`{ type: "component", answer }`, the answer schema of its kind), skips one (`{ type: "component", skip: true }`; the stored part keeps no answer or grade and is marked `skipped`), or answers a confirmation (`{ type: "confirmation", confirmed }`) and streams the resumed turn. While other questions of the message's [practice set](#practice-sets) are still open, the stream is only `answered` and `done` with the stored message, still `awaiting_user`; the set's last answer streams the resumed turn. `404 TOOL_CALL_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown tool call, `400 ANSWER_INVALID` for an answer of another kind (or a skip of a confirmation) or one that does not fit (a wrong number of blanks or nouns, chunks that are not the offered ones), `409 TOOL_CALL_ANSWERED` for a second answer (also when two arrive at once), `409 CONVERSATION_ARCHIVED`, and `429 AI_DAILY_LIMIT`.
- `POST .../messages/:messageId/speech` with `{ spanIndex }`, or `{ toolCallId }` for a Say it or Dictation sentence, returns the clip; see [on-demand audio](speech.md#on-demand-audio).
- `GET` and `PUT /api/ai/profile` read and update the [tutor profile](#tutor-profile) (`{ nativeLanguage, replyLanguage, goals }`; empty goals mean none, and the notes cannot be written by the learner); `DELETE /api/ai/profile/notes` clears the tutor notes. Each answers `{ profile }`; a missing profile reads as the defaults with `updatedAt: null`.

**Streamed turns.** A turn answers `text/event-stream`; each event's name is its `type` and its data the JSON event (`tutorStreamEventSchema`). The order is: `user_message` (a new message only, with the stored user message) or `answered` (a respond only, with the answered component or card and its grade or outcome), then `start` with the new assistant message's ID, then any number of `text` deltas, `tool_call` and `tool_result` steps, at most one `component` or `confirmation` (the turn then waits for the learner), an `error` (`AI_UNAVAILABLE`, `AI_REQUEST_FAILED`, or `TURN_LIMIT`) when the turn failed, and always `done` with the stored assistant message last. A new text part begins after each step. The stored message is the source of truth: a client that goes away does not stop the turn, which is stored when it ends, also when it failed (status `error`, with the parts so far and an error part).

C14l's routes (plans, context scope, notes, tutor words) are listed under [tutor sessions and memory](tutor-sessions-and-memory.md#api).

Data lives in [`ai_conversations`](data-model.md#ai_conversations), [`ai_messages`](data-model.md#ai_messages), [`learner_profiles`](data-model.md#learner_profiles), and [`ai_usage`](data-model.md#ai_usage), with the learning tables listed under [learning data](data-model.md#learning-data).

## Not in scope

- Journal posts and comments as tutor context.
- Voice conversation (speaking whole turns to the tutor); only components listen.
- Sharing conversations, or showing them to course owners or the superuser. The superuser sees counts and costs, never messages.
- Ranking, streaks, or scores. The tutor's feedback stays personal, like the rest of Wordinator.
