## 19-a Ik hou van zwemmen
- **Grammar**:
  - houden van + noun — basic — "Ik hou van muziek." "Piotr houdt van voetbal."; van is never left out; ik hou / ik houd (both standard, hou more common in speech), jij/u/hij/zij houdt (dt spelling), wij/jullie/zij houden; t-drop when je or jij follows the verb, whether or not jij is written out: "Hou je / Houd je / Hou jij / Houd jij …?" are all fine, never *Houdt jij*.
  - houden van + infinitive (activity) — basic — "Ik hou van zwemmen."; the infinitive used as a noun is a het-word (het zwemmen) and can be the subject: "Zwemmen is gezond." "Koken is leuk."
  - vinden + activity/noun + adjective — practiced — "Ik vind tekenen leuk." "Piotr vindt Nederlands moeilijk." "Vind je voetbal saai?"; adjective last, no -e (predicative, 17-b); vindt with -dt; extends the chunk "Ik vind … leuk/lekker".
  - houden van + person — intro — "Ik hou van mijn familie." = family or romantic love; no object pronouns (21-b).
  - Questions about free time — basic — "Wat doe je in je vrije tijd?" "Wat is je hobby?" "Heb je een hobby?" "Wat is je lievelingsfilm?" (lievelings- + noun, one word).
  - al + period + present tense — intro (chunk) — "Ik woon al twee jaar in Utrecht." = "I have lived …"; Dutch keeps the present.
  - Mistakes addressed: *Ik hou zwemmen* → Ik hou van zwemmen; *hij houd* → hij houdt.

- **Story facts**: Tuesday 17 November, Dutch class 19.00–21.00 at the library in the centre; Sofia cycles there. Marieke (de docent) asks about hobbies. Sofia loves swimming (alone, not in a club, Wednesdays at 7.00) and dancing (at home, not in a club); she finds games (spelletjes) a bit boring. Piotr loves football and cooking: he plays in a small amateur club with a match every Saturday, FC Utrecht is his favourite club (lievelingsclub) and he watches football on TV on Sundays; he finds Dutch hard, especially de/het; he does puzzles and plays games with his colleagues in the break. Piotr has lived in Utrecht for two years and likes it, and so does Kasia. Kasia loves music: she plays guitar and sings in a choir (accepted, coordinator decision #2), finds football boring. Piotr and Kasia sometimes go to concerts. A Cursist loves drawing. After class Sofia and Piotr talk at the bike racks (Sofia with her black bike).
- **Deviations from brief**: none of substance. "Piotr plays in an amateur club with Saturday matches" is an elaboration of "loves football (FC Utrecht)"; FC Utrecht is only his favourite club. Extra glossed words beyond the brief list: praten, spelen, makkelijk, de vereniging. Marieke's "Zeg maar jij." recycle not used.
