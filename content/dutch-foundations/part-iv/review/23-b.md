## 23-b Dat hoeft niet
- **Grammar**:
  - *hoeven* — practiced — in statements, use it with a negative or restrictive word to say something is not necessary; questions may ask directly whether something is necessary; ik hoef, jij/u/hij/zij hoeft, wij/jullie/zij hoeven, and *hoef je …?* has t-drop.
  - *hoeven + niet/geen/niets/nooit/alleen + te + infinitive* — practiced — use *te* before an ordinary infinitive and between the prefix and verb in a separable infinitive (*mee te nemen, op te staan, in te pakken*).
  - *moeten* vs *niet hoeven* vs *niet mogen* — practiced — *moeten* expresses necessity, *niet hoeven* means something is unnecessary, and *niet mogen* means it is forbidden.
  - *hoeven* without an infinitive — basic — *Dat hoeft niet* is a short response; *geen* can precede a noun phrase (*Ik hoef geen koffie*).
  - *alleen + hoeven* — basic — *alleen* limits what is necessary (*Je hoeft alleen de planten water te geven*).
- **Vocabulary** (18 words, 8 blocks): *hoeven*; *Dat hoeft niet.*; *niets*; *de koffer*; *water geven*; *de post*; *de reservesleutel*; *de keer*; *per week*; *pakken*; *hoor* (new sentence-final softening use; *Nee hoor* known from 07-b); *op vakantie gaan*; *de gieter*; *sluiten*; *openen*; *bewaren*; *de oplader*; *de toilettas*.
- **Expressions**: *Moet ik iets meenemen?*; *Je hoeft niets mee te nemen!*; *Nee hoor.*; *Dat hoeft niet.*; *Hoe vaak is genoeg?*; *Wat lief!*; *Ja, graag.*; *Je hoeft me niets te betalen, hoor!*; *Dank je wel.*
- **Story facts**: On Thursday 10 December, Sofia's guests ask what to bring to her Saturday 12 December housewarming; she says they do not need to bring anything, while Henk brings cheese. On Friday 11 December, Henk offers to water Sofia's plants and collect her post while she is away. He will water the plants twice a week, take in the post, and receives her spare key. Sofia does not need to pay him.
